The soldered half: how those five parts connect

Only the components with soldered or clamp connections: the battery and BMS, the KEY socket, the breaker, the splice point and the gun breakout. Each plug that hangs off them is drawn as the half you built (a female stub, or the key socket's male face), but not the thing it plugs into. The voltmeter, bucks, sensor and guns are gone: only their plug ends are left.

Is it a closed loop?

Not on its own. It's one connected chain, and the circle only closes through the loads. Follow the current out and back:

pack + → KEY pin A → (the key's U-loop) → pin B → breaker → + splice → out a plug into a load → back along that plug's − wire → − splice → BMS P− → through the BMS → B− → pack −.

Charging is a second loop that doesn't use the key: pack + → charger lead → charger → back to P− → through the BMS → pack −.

The three open gaps

  1. The key. Pin A and pin B are separate pins. Only the key's U-loop joins them, so with the key out, nothing downstream has power.
  2. Every plug. The loads are on the far side of the XT30s and the XT60H.
  3. The sensor. The only thing between the + splice and the gun breakout is the sensor's own conductor, which isn't on this page. If you ever find a wire joining those two nodes, that's a bug: it bypasses the sensor.

Meter checks before you plug anything in

Key out, breaker on:

  • + splice to − splice: no beep.
  • Gun breakout to + splice, and to − splice: no beep.
  • Charger lead + to −: reads the pack voltage, 7.2–8.4 V. It's live even with the key out.

Key in, breaker on:

  • + splice to − splice reads 7.2–8.4 V. If it reads 0 V, check that the key's U-loop is seated, the breaker is on, and give the BMS a few seconds on the charger to wake.
  • Gun breakout to − splice still reads 0 V, since it's only fed through the sensor.

Wiring the breaker's clamps

No ring lugs here. They're for threaded studs, and your breaker has built-in wire clamps. The wire goes in bare.

  • Strip only as much as the clamp is deep, so no bare copper shows outside it.
  • Best: a crimp ferrule on the stripped end (the 4 mm² size is the right one for 12 AWG). Without one, twist the strands tight and fold the tip back on itself so the clamp grips a thicker bundle.
  • Don't tin the end with solder. Solder squashes under a clamp and the joint works loose.
  • Tighten, give each wire a tug, and re-tighten after the first few runs: fine silicone-wire strands settle.
  • Don't solder to the breaker itself. The heat damages its internal contacts.
  • If the terminals are marked (BAT or LINE), the key side goes on that one.

Always live

KEY socket pin A and the charger lead are live with the key out. The female plugs are recessed so nothing is exposed, but pin A isn't. Put the key blank in the socket whenever the key is out.
