Plug in and test, one step at a time

Everything soldered is done, so the rest is plugging in. Same plug map as page 4: green ✓ marks what already works, and the yellow numbers are the order to plug in the rest. Each step has a quick check before you move on, so if something's wrong you know exactly which plug did it.

Every time: key out before you plug or unplug anything, then key in to test. If anything gets hot, smells, or the breaker trips, pull the key first and look second.

✓Already done

Plug

  • Battery and BMS, the KEY socket and key, the breaker, the splice and the gun breakout.
  • Voltmeter: its XT30 male into the splice's XT30 female. It reads the pack with the key in, so the key, the breaker and the + side of the splice all work.

1Buck A input

Plug

  • Buck A's input XT30 male into the splice's Buck A lead (XT30 female).
  • Leave its output lead loose: don't plug J1 yet.

Then check

  • Key in. Meter Buck A's output lead (the male JST pins): 4.75–5.25 V, ideally 5.0 V. If it has a trimmer, set it now.
  • Key out.

If not: Over 5.25 V: don't go on. The control board's ceiling is 5.25 V.

2Control board power

Plug

  • Buck A's output 2-pin JST male into the board's J1 pigtail (2-pin JST female).
  • J2 and J3 stay unplugged.

Then check

  • Key in. Green POWER on, yellow GUN off, red PUMP off.
  • TP2 reads about 5.0 V. TP11 (pump gate) reads under 0.05 V.
  • Press PRIME: yellow and red come on. Let go: red stays on for a moment (the delay), then goes off.

If not: TP11 over 0.05 V at rest, or red PUMP on by itself: key out and stop.

3Pump

Plug

  • The pump lead's 2-pin JST male into the board's J3 pigtail (2-pin JST female).

Then check

  • Key in. The pump stays off at rest.
  • Hold PRIME: it runs. Let go: it runs on for the delay, then stops.
  • Check it pumps from intake to outlet. Set the run-on with RV2 (DELAY): clockwise is longer, about 350 ms to start.

If not: Pumps backwards: swap the two wires at the motor tabs, and move the diode so its band stays on the tab the red wire now goes to.

4Current sensor

Plug

  • The sensor's XT60H male into the splice's sensor lead (XT60H female).
  • The board's J2 plug (3-pin JST female) onto the sensor's signal pins (3-pin male header).

Then check

  • Key in, no guns plugged in: yellow GUN stays off.
  • TP5 (the sensor signal) reads about 2.50 V.
  • Set RV1 (THRESH) so TP6 reads about 50 mV above TP5 to start. Clockwise raises it.

If not: GUN on with nothing plugged in: turn RV1 clockwise until it goes off.

5Gun ports and a gun

Plug

  • Inside, at the bulkhead: each gun port's XT30 male pigtail into its splice gun lead (XT30 female).
  • Outside: the umbilical's pack-end male into the port, its gun-end female onto the gun.

Then check

  • Key in. Fire the gun: TP5 rises, yellow GUN and red PUMP come on, and the pump runs. Let go: the pump stops after the delay.
  • Tune RV1: put TP6 between the resting and firing TP5 readings, at least 10 mV from each (30–50 mV apart is better).

If not: GUN never lights: watch TP5 while firing. If it drops below 2.5 V, the sensor is backwards: swap the two big wires at its terminals.

6Buck B and the LED backpack

Plug

  • Buck B's input XT30 male into the splice's Buck B lead (XT30 female).

Then check

  • Key in. The LED 5V USB-A port on the tube wall gives 5 V. Plug the backpack in from outside.

7Charger

Plug

  • The charger's output XT30 male into the battery's charger lead (XT30 female).

Then check

  • Plug USB-C into the CHG port: the charger's light comes on. It works with the key out.
  • With the key in, the voltmeter climbs slowly while it charges.

Test points are the small labelled holes on the control board: TP2 is the 5 V logic rail, TP5 the sensor signal, TP6 the RV1 threshold, and TP11 the pump gate. Meter them against any ground, such as the − splice or the board's J1 black wire. Page 2 shows where each one is.
