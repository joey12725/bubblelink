Must fix · SW1 PRIME

Fitted as the board is drawn, PRIME is always pressed. The board uses the two bottom holes: DET (square) and GND. Omron's datasheet shows the two legs straight across the body (6.5 mm apart) are one piece of metal inside the switch, so DET is tied to GND permanently. The switch only fits the holes one way, so you can't just rotate it.

  1. Check first: with the switch unpressed, beep the legs. The pair that beeps should be straight across the body. If it's instead the two legs on the same side, skip this fix.
  2. Cut off the leg that would go into hole 3 (bottom-right, GND) flush with the body.
  3. Fit the switch flat and solder holes 1, 2 and 4.
  4. On the back, solder a short wire (a clipped LED leg) from hole 4 to hole 3. Hole 3 is still GND, and hole 4 is connected to nothing else.
  5. Test: J4's two empty holes are DET and GND. They should be open, and beep only while you press. Powered, the yellow GUN LED should light only while you press. If it's on all the time, this is why.
Heads-up · RV1 / RV2 pin order

The board and the repo docs assume the 3296W's legs run 1-3-2. Bourns' current datasheet shows 1-2-3, wiper in the middle, with the screw at the pin-1 end. Fit both trimmers with the screw over the square pad. That also lines the screw up with the cap's adjustment window.

  1. RV2 (delay): the board ties the middle and right holes together, so it still works as a 0–100k variable resistor. Clockwise still gives a longer delay. No change.
  2. RV1 (threshold): still works and clockwise still raises it, but the range is about 2.50–2.79 V instead of 2.21–2.79 V. The lost bottom part is below the sensor's 2.5 V zero point, the "pump always on" zone. At bring-up, full counterclockwise will read about 2.50 V at TP6, not 2.21 V.
  3. Check with a meter before soldering: the two end legs read a fixed 2k or 100k, and the middle leg changes as you turn the screw.
  4. The trimmer bodies overhang R2 (under RV1) and R9 (under RV2), and half cover TP6 and TP9. Let them rest on those resistors rather than forcing them flat. Probe TP6 and TP9 from the back of the board; the holes go all the way through.
Fit · heights matter in the cap

The board hangs parts-up under the cap, so a few heights are set by the printed parts:

  1. LEDs: flange flat on the board. The cap allows 5.3 mm, and its light tunnel starts 1 mm above that.
  2. SW1: flat on the board (4.3 mm). The PRIME plunger rests 0.3 mm above it, so a switch sitting high is held pressed.
  3. JST headers: the latch side can face either way. When you crimp, put the empty plug on the header and note which hole sits over the square pad. Red goes there on all three: +5 V on J1 and J2, PUMP+ on J3.
  4. Easiest order is lowest first: SW1, LEDs, C1, trimmers, headers. Then do the SW1 back jumper.
