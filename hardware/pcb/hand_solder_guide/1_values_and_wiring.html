What value goes where, and which wire goes on which pin

Board top view, to scale. Pin 1 of every connector is the yellow square pad. The wires show where each one leaves the board.

RV1 · THRESH2 kΩ25-turn trimmer, printed 202. Left position. Screw over the square pad.
RV2 · DELAY100 kΩ25-turn trimmer, printed 104. Right position. Screw over the square pad.
C1 · on the board100 µF16 V electrolytic, 5 mm × 11 mm. Long leg (+) in the square pad.
AT THE PUMP MOTOR100 nFCeramic, ≥25 V, X7R. Printed 104. Soldered straight across the two motor tabs.
AT THE PUMP MOTORSchottky diode2–3 A, ≥20 V, e.g. 1N5822 (3 A, 40 V). Band (cathode) to motor +.
Don't mix up the two "104"s. On a blue trimmer, 104 means 100 kΩ (RV2). On a small ceramic disc, 104 means 100 nF (the pump-motor cap). And 202 means 2 kΩ (RV1).

Set Buck A to 5.0 V with a meter before you plug it in. The board's limit is 5.25 V.

J2 is mirrored compared with J1 and J3: pin 1 is on the right. Check the pin names printed on your module. Keep this cable away from the pump wires.

J3 · pump 2-pin XH, twisted pair to the motor

PinPadWireSignalOther end

Both the 100 nF cap and the diode solder straight across the motor tabs, legs as short as possible. Twist the red and black pair. Neither part is in the order ledger, so buy them if you don't have them.

Wire colours are a suggestion. The pin numbers and pads are what count. If your pre-crimped JST leads are all one colour, mark pin 1 with a red pen or a bit of red heat-shrink. To find pin 1 on a plug, push the empty plug onto its header and see which hole sits over the square pad.
