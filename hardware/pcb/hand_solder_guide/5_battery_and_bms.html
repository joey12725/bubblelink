Battery and BMS: where every battery wire is soldered

Both holder faces, looking straight at them from outside. Find the engraved letters: A marks row A, B row B, T the TERM face and M the MID face. Dots show where each wire lands. Solder to a tab, never straight onto a cell.

Main − comes off P−, never the battery

Every return in the stick goes to the BMS P− pad. If anything is wired straight to the row A − bus, the BMS can't cut it off in a short or a flat battery. The build guide's line "P− (row 1 −)" is misleading: P− is the BMS pad, not the battery's − bus.

Order and checks

  1. Tabs first (TERM, then MID), then the two meter checks from the battery guide.
  2. BMS wires in this order: B−, then BM, then B+.
  3. Then main +, main − and the charger pair.
  4. Check: main + to P− reads 7.2–8.4 V. If it reads 0 V, many BMS boards need a few seconds on the charger to wake up.

Why the middle cell

With the thick wires on the middle cell's tab, current reaches it from both directions, so no single tab carries more than about two cells' worth. If you have spare tabs, double up the two links on each side of those points.

If your BMS has a P+ pad, beep it to B+. On these 2S boards they're the same point, so leave P+ empty and take main + off the bus as shown. If P+ doesn't beep to B+, stop and check before going further.
