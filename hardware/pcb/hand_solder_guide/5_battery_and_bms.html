Main − comes off P−, never the battery

Every return in the stick goes to the BMS's big P− pad. If anything is wired straight to the negative rail, the BMS can't cut it off in a short or a flat battery. The build guide's line "P− (row 1 −)" is misleading: P− is the BMS pad, not the battery's − bus.

Your BMS's six connections

  • Big B− (pad with a hole): the negative rail, 12 AWG. The whole load current flows through it.
  • Big P− (the other pad with a hole): main − and the charger's −. The BMS switches between B− and P− to cut everything off, so never join them.
  • B+ (round pad by the connector): a thin red to the positive rail. B2's short wire joins it.
  • P+ (round pad next to it): leave empty. Main + goes straight from the positive rail to KEY pin A.
  • Balance plug (3-pin JST): black = little B−, cut short and soldered to the big B− pad; middle red = B1 to the bottom; outer red = B2, cut short and soldered to the B+ pad.

The label's 同口（带均衡） means "common port, with balancing": charging and the loads share P−, exactly as wired here.

Order and checks

  1. Tabs first (top, then bottom), then the two meter checks from the battery guide.
  2. Before you cut anything, beep the end of the B2 wire to the B+ pad, and the end of the black wire to the big B− pad. Beeps mean they're already the same points on the board, so the short wires just keep live wires from dangling.
  3. Cut both short and solder them to those pads.
  4. Connect from lowest to highest. First the big B− (negative rail), then the middle red, B1 (bottom). Check before going on: the negative rail to the bottom reads one cell, 3.6–4.2 V.
  5. The B+ pad's thin red goes last (positive rail). Then the negative rail to the B+ pad reads the whole pack, 7.2–8.4 V. The balance plug can go into the BMS any time before this step.
  6. Then main +, main − and the charger pair.
  7. Check: main + to P− reads 7.2–8.4 V. If it reads 0 V, many BMS boards need a few seconds on the charger to wake up.
  8. Beep B+ to P+ on the board. A beep means they're one point and the BMS only switches the − side, as drawn. If it doesn't beep, stop and tell me.

Joining B2 to B+ and the little B− to the big B−

Both are fine, and they save two wires to the battery. B2 and B+ are both the top of the stack, and the little B− and the big B− are both the bottom, so the BMS gets the same signals either way.

Neither connects the BMS to the battery by itself: the big B− and the B+ pad each still need their own wire to the rails, and now carry the sensing too, so make those joints solid and keep them from being pulled.

One difference for B−: the big B− wire carries the whole load current, so the BMS now senses the bottom cell at the pad instead of at the battery. A few thousandths of an ohm in the wire and joints at 30 A is tens of millivolts. It reads in the safe direction (the low-voltage cutoff comes slightly early, never late), so it's harmless. Keep that wire short and thick.

Why the middle cell

With the thick wires on the middle cell's tab, current reaches it from both directions, so no single tab carries more than about two cells' worth. If you have spare tabs, double up the two links on each side of those points.
